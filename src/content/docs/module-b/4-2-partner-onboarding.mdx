## What the Auditor Checks

The auditor verifies that your organisation can onboard customers into AI workloads in a structured way and leave them able to operate the solution. That means more than a slide deck: they want repeatable templates, operational guides, and enablement material.

A strong package shows a standard engagement kit covering discovery, design, delivery cadence, operational readiness, Knowledge Transfer, and post-go-live support.

## Required Evidence Checklist

| # | Evidence Item | Accepted Formats | Status |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Customer onboarding pack or welcome kit | PDF, PowerPoint | ⬜ |
| 2 | At least two reusable delivery templates used in AI engagements | PDF, Word, PowerPoint | ⬜ |
| 3 | Knowledge Transfer plan template | PDF, Word | ⬜ |
| 4 | Customer-facing runbook or operational guide, anonymised if needed | PDF, Word | ⬜ |
| 5 | Training materials or recording index used during handover | PDF, PowerPoint | ⬜ |

## Implementation Guidance

### Design the onboarding pack

- Explain who the customer team will work with, what the delivery cadence looks like, and what approvals are expected.
- Document environment standards such as naming, tagging, access, data classification, and release management.
- Include support boundaries so the customer knows what changes remain in-scope after go-live.

### Create reusable delivery assets

- Maintain templates for architecture, sprint reporting, testing, go-live, and operational handover.
- Keep customer-specific content out of the template master so the asset remains reusable for audit evidence.
- Show how onboarding templates connect to the runbook, project plan, and Knowledge Transfer plan.

### Prove operational enablement

- Include runbooks, dashboard links, escalation paths, and role-based training material in the handover package.
- Use a sign-off or exit assessment to prove the receiving team can operate the workload.
- Track recordings and supporting artefacts in one indexed handover location.

## What the Auditor Checks

The auditor verifies that at least three unique customers contribute to the qualifying Azure Consumed Revenue window, proving market breadth rather than concentration in one flagship account.

They also want to see that those customers are backed by eligible association types and that the contribution can be traced to the same qualifying pillars used for the Azure Consumed Revenue thresholds.

## Required Evidence Checklist

| # | Evidence Item | Accepted Formats | Status |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Customer diversity table with at least three unique customers | Excel, PDF | ⬜ |
| 2 | Association type evidence for each counted customer | PDF, screenshot | ⬜ |
| 3 | Pillar alignment per customer showing where the qualifying revenue sits | Excel, PDF | ⬜ |
| 4 | Optional industry or workload summary to demonstrate breadth | Excel, PDF | ⬜ |

## Implementation Guidance

### Create the customer evidence table

- Use consistent customer identifiers so the same account can be matched across revenue, case studies, and references.
- Show the association type for every counted customer and note if one customer spans multiple pillars.
- If you anonymise customer names, keep the anonymisation consistent across the full evidence package.

### Validate uniqueness

- Count customers, not subscriptions or association records; multiple subscriptions for one customer still count once.
- Cross-check the audit period carefully because a customer outside the three-month window cannot be included.
- Use the Partner Center customer view to prove the contribution source before exporting the final table.

### Use diversity strategically

- Where possible, align the three customers with different industries or workload types to show delivery breadth.
- Link customer diversity evidence to case studies or references so the control supports the overall partner story.
- Keep a pipeline view of near-qualifying accounts in case the auditor questions whether a customer truly counts.

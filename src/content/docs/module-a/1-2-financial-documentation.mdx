## What the Auditor Checks

The auditor checks that the partner is financially viable enough to deliver the contracted services and carries appropriate insurance for professional risk. They do not need every internal finance detail, but they do need enough evidence to establish stability.

## Required Evidence Checklist

| # | Evidence Item | Accepted Formats | Status |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Most recent signed or audited financial statements | PDF | ⬜ |
| 2 | Professional indemnity or errors and omissions insurance certificate | PDF | ⬜ |
| 3 | Public liability insurance certificate, where applicable | PDF | ⬜ |
| 4 | No insolvency or bankruptcy declaration, if requested by the auditor | PDF, signed letter | ⬜ |

## Quick Guidance

- If you are part of a group, add a one-page explanation of which entity contracts the work and which entity owns the financial statements.
- Check that the insured entity name exactly matches the legal name used in A.1.1.
- Keep the policy period visible; expired insurance certificates are frequently rejected.
- If you redact sensitive figures, leave total revenue and profitability visible so viability can still be assessed.
- Prepare one sentence explaining any unusual finance structure before the kickoff call.

<Aside type="caution" title="Top gaps to avoid">
  Do not submit insurance in a trading name that differs from the legal entity, and do not rely on financial statements that are so old the auditor cannot judge current viability.
</Aside>
